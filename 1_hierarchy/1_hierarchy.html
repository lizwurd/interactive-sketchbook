Fourth Wing

by Rebecca Yarros

Conscription Day is always the deadliest. Maybe that’s why the
sunrise is especially beautiful this morning—because I know it
might be my last.

I tighten the straps of my heavy canvas rucksack and trudge up
the wide staircase of the stone fortress I call home. My chest heaves
with exertion, my lungs burning by the time I reach the stone
corridor leading to General Sorrengail’s office. This is what six
months of intense physical training has given me—the ability to
barely climb six flights of stairs with a thirty-pound pack.

I’m so fucked.

The thousands of twenty-year-olds waiting outside the gate to
enter their chosen quadrant for service are the smartest and
strongest in Navarre. Hundreds of them have been preparing for
the Riders Quadrant, the chance to become one of the elite, since
birth. I’ve had exactly six months.


Reflection


This exercise got me more comfortable with using tags. I was
moving around very slowly, but I believe with time, I'll get more
comfortable with HTML. Hierarchy is a must in design, so my prior
knowledge did help a bit. I do feel that it looks crazy because I
tried using all the required tags.. I think that shows more than
anything. But it changed the flow of how the reader would view the
passage. Starting off strong for sure. I'm not super satisfied with
it because it doesn't look "organized." But more importantly, I'm trying
to focus on the familiarity of these tags. If I had more time, I
would add more style and color.. But I have to walk before I run
right? Through this exercise, I can definitely see how the delivery
of information is up to the designer. My delivery is chaotic, but
a more pleasing design can come later.
